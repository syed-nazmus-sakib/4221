The full question

Fresh regression practice · Problem 3 · 2 → 3 → 1 · tanh hidden, linear output

Consider a network \(2\rightarrow3\rightarrow1\) with Tanh hidden units and a linear output. Given:

\[ x=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad y=0.8 \] \[ W^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.4 & -0.2 \\ 0.2 & -0.1 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad b^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ W^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ -0.4 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad b^{(2)}=0.1, \qquad \eta=0.1 \]

with the loss

\[ \boxed{ L=\tfrac12(\hat y-y)^{2} } \]

Compute:

  • (a)The complete forward pass — \(z^{(1)}\), \(h\) and \(\hat y\).
  • (b)The loss.
  • (c)The output delta and the gradients of \(W^{(2)},b^{(2)}\).
  • (d)The tanh derivative at each hidden unit.
  • (e)The hidden delta and the gradients of \(W^{(1)},b^{(1)}\).
  • (f)The updated parameters.

The network at a glance

Before solving: what tanh changes

The output side is identical to ANN-6 — linear output, \(\tfrac12\) loss — so \(\delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y\) again. The only new machinery is the hidden activation:

\[ \boxed{ \tanh'(z)=1-\tanh^{2}(z)=1-h^{2} } \]

Two practical consequences, both different from ReLU:

  • Tanh never dies. A negative pre-activation gives a negative activation, not a zero — so every hidden unit keeps learning.
  • But the derivative is always less than 1 (it peaks at 1 when \(z=0\)), so every layer it passes through shrinks the gradient. That is the vanishing-gradient problem in miniature.
   x (2)             h (3)              y^ (1)
  ┌─────┐  W(1)ᵀ    ┌──────┐   W(2)ᵀ   ┌────────┐
  │  1  │ ────────► │ tanh │ ────────► │ linear │ ──► y^ ──► ½(y^ − y)² ──► L
  │  1  │  + b(1)   │ tanh │   + b(2)  └────────┘         y = 0.8
  └─────┘           │ tanh │
                    └──────┘
       2×3                  3×1     backward: multiply by (1 − h²)

Because \(h=\tanh(z)\) is already computed in the forward pass, use \(1-h^{2}\) rather than recomputing \(\tanh\) — it is the same number and far less work.

(a) Forward pass

\[ (W^{(1)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.2 \\ 0.4 & -0.1 \\ -0.2 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix} \qquad z^{(1)}=(W^{(1)})^{T}x+b^{(1)} \]

With \(x=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\) each pre-activation is simply the sum of a column of \(W^{(1)}\) plus the bias:

\[ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.3+0.2+0 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.5 } \] \[ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.4-0.1+0.2 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.5 } \] \[ z^{(1)}_3 = -0.2-0.3+0 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_3 = -0.5 } \]
\[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0.5 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix} \]

Apply tanh

Tanh is an odd function, \(\tanh(-z)=-\tanh(z)\), so one evaluation covers all three:

\[ \tanh(0.5)\approx 0.4621, \qquad \tanh(-0.5)\approx -0.4621 \]
\[ \boxed{ h=\begin{bmatrix} 0.4621 \\ 0.4621 \\ -0.4621 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Linear output

\[ \hat y = 0.5(0.4621)-0.4(0.4621)+0.2(-0.4621)+0.1 \] \[ = 0.4621\,(0.5-0.4-0.2)+0.1 = 0.4621(-0.1)+0.1 = -0.04621+0.1 \]
\[ \boxed{ \hat y \approx 0.05379 } \]

Factoring out the shared \(0.4621\) turns three multiplications into one — worth doing whenever the activations repeat.

(b) Loss

\[ L=\tfrac12(0.05379-0.8)^{2}=\tfrac12(-0.74621)^{2}=\tfrac12(0.55683) \]
\[ \boxed{ L \approx 0.27842 } \]

The prediction \(0.054\) is a long way below the target \(0.8\), so expect a large delta.

(c) Output delta and output gradients

Linear output, \(\tfrac12\) loss — the same clean case as ANN-6:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y } = 0.05379-0.8 \]
\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)} \approx -0.74621 } \]
\[ \nabla W^{(2)}=h\,\delta^{(2)} =\begin{bmatrix} 0.4621 \\ 0.4621 \\ -0.4621 \end{bmatrix}(-0.74621) \]
\[ \boxed{ \nabla W^{(2)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.34484 \\ -0.34484 \\ 0.34484 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \nabla b^{(2)} = \delta^{(2)} \approx -0.74621 } \]

All three magnitudes are equal because all three \(|h_i|\) are equal; only the third sign flips, because \(h_3\) is negative.

(d) The tanh derivative

\[ \boxed{ \tanh'(z)=1-\tanh^{2}(z)=1-h^{2} } \]

Every \(|h_i|\approx0.4621\), and squaring kills the sign, so all three derivatives are identical:

\[ 1-(0.4621)^{2} = 1-0.21355 \]
\[ \boxed{ \tanh'(z^{(1)}) \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.78645 \\ 0.78645 \\ 0.78645 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Note it is \(<1\): this factor shrinks every gradient that passes back through the hidden layer by about \(21\%\). Stack many tanh layers and the gradient vanishes — the reason ReLU became the default.

(e) Hidden delta and first-layer gradients

First push the error back through \(W^{(2)}\):

\[ W^{(2)}\delta^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ -0.4 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix}(-0.74621) \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.37311 \\ 0.29848 \\ -0.14924 \end{bmatrix} \]

Then multiply element-wise by the tanh derivative:

\[ \delta^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} -0.37311 \\ 0.29848 \\ -0.14924 \end{bmatrix}\odot \begin{bmatrix} 0.78645 \\ 0.78645 \\ 0.78645 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 \\ 0.23474 \\ -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \]

First-layer gradients

\[ \nabla W^{(1)}=x\,(\delta^{(1)})^{T}, \qquad x=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Both input components are 1, so both rows are copies of \((\delta^{(1)})^{T}\):

\[ \boxed{ \nabla W^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 & 0.23474 & -0.11737 \\ -0.29343 & 0.23474 & -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \nabla b^{(1)} = \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 \\ 0.23474 \\ -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \]

(f) Updated parameters

\[ \theta_{\text{new}}=\theta_{\text{old}}-\eta\nabla\theta, \qquad \eta=0.1 \]

Output layer

\[ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5+0.034484 \\ -0.4+0.034484 \\ 0.2-0.034484 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=0.1+0.074621 \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.53448 \\ -0.36552 \\ 0.16552 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx 0.17462 } \]

Hidden layer

\[ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3+0.029343 & 0.4-0.023474 & -0.2+0.011737 \\ 0.2+0.029343 & -0.1-0.023474 & -0.3+0.011737 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.32934 & 0.37653 & -0.18826 \\ 0.22934 & -0.12347 & -0.28826 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.02934 \\ 0.17653 \\ 0.01174 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Every parameter moved, unlike ANN-6 where the dead ReLU unit froze a whole column. This is tanh's advantage.

Final answer sheet

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0.5 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ h \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4621 \\ 0.4621 \\ -0.4621 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \hat y \approx 0.05379 } \qquad \boxed{ L \approx 0.27842 } \] \[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)} \approx -0.74621 } \qquad \boxed{ \nabla W^{(2)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.34484 \\ -0.34484 \\ 0.34484 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \nabla b^{(2)} \approx -0.74621 } \] \[ \boxed{ \tanh' \approx 0.78645 \text{ (all three)} } \qquad \boxed{ \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 \\ 0.23474 \\ -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ \nabla W^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 & 0.23474 & -0.11737 \\ -0.29343 & 0.23474 & -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \nabla b^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.29343 \\ 0.23474 \\ -0.11737 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.53448 \\ -0.36552 \\ 0.16552 \end{bmatrix} } \quad \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx 0.17462 } \] \[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.32934 & 0.37653 & -0.18826 \\ 0.22934 & -0.12347 & -0.28826 \end{bmatrix} } \quad \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.02934 \\ 0.17653 \\ 0.01174 \end{bmatrix} } \]

The one extra factor

\[ \boxed{ \text{Backpropagating through tanh multiplies by } 1-h^{2} } \]
