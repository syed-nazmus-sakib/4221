A \(3\rightarrow2\rightarrow1\) network with ReLU in the hidden layer and Sigmoid at the output. Forward pass, BCE loss for \(y=0\), full gradients for both layers, then one gradient-descent update with \(\eta=0.1\).

The full question

3 inputs · 2 hidden (ReLU) · 1 output (Sigmoid) · BCE loss · y = 0 · η = 0.1
\[ x=\begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix} \qquad W^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & -0.4 \\ 0.8 & 0.3 \\ -0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ W^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(2)}=-0.05 \]

Hidden activation: ReLU. Output activation: Sigmoid. Target: \(y=0\). Learning rate: \(\eta=0.1\).

Answer the following:

  • (a)Compute the hidden-layer activations.
  • (b)Compute the output-layer activation.
  • (c)Compute the BCE loss.
  • (d)Compute the gradients with respect to \(W^{(2)}\) and \(b^{(2)}\).
  • (e)Compute the gradients with respect to \(W^{(1)}\) and \(b^{(1)}\).
  • (f)Update \(W^{(2)}\) and \(b^{(2)}\).
  • (g)Update \(W^{(1)}\) and \(b^{(1)}\).

The network at a glance

First: understand the matrix direction

We have \(W^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}\), because there are 3 inputs and 2 hidden neurons. So \((W^{(1)})^{T}\) has shape \(2\times3\), which is what lines up with \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\).

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=(W^{(1)})^{T}x+b^{(1)} } \qquad \boxed{ h=\mathrm{ReLU}(z^{(1)}) } \] \[ \boxed{ z^{(2)}=(W^{(2)})^{T}h+b^{(2)} } \qquad \boxed{ \hat y=\sigma(z^{(2)}) } \] 
  x (3)            h (2)            y^ (1)
 ┌─────┐  W(1)ᵀ   ┌─────┐  W(2)ᵀ   ┌─────┐
 │ 0.6 │ ───────► │ ReLU│ ───────► │ σ   │ ──► y^ ──► BCE ──► L
 │ 0.9 │  + b(1)  │ ReLU│  + b(2)  └─────┘             (y = 0)
 │ 0.4 │          └─────┘
 └─────┘
      3×2                 2×1

Getting this orientation right is half the marks — every gradient shape follows from it.

(a) Hidden-layer activations

\[ (W^{(1)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.8 & -0.3 \\ -0.4 & 0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.8 & -0.3 \\ -0.4 & 0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Hidden neuron 1

\[ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.3(0.6)+0.8(0.9)-0.3(0.4)+0.2 \] \[ = 0.18+0.72-0.12+0.2 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.98 } \]

Hidden neuron 2

\[ z^{(1)}_2 = -0.4(0.6)+0.3(0.9)+0.2(0.4)-0.1 \] \[ = -0.24+0.27+0.08-0.1 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.01 } \]

Now apply ReLU, \(\mathrm{ReLU}(z)=\max(0,z)\). Both values are positive, so both pass through unchanged:

\[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{ h=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Worth noting for part (e): because both pre-activations are \(>0\), no ReLU unit is dead, so the ReLU derivative will be \(1\) for both.

(b) Output activation

\[ z^{(2)}=(W^{(2)})^{T}h+b^{(2)} =\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix}-0.05 \] \[ = 0.294+0.004-0.05 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)} = 0.248 } \]

Now the sigmoid, \(\sigma(z)=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-z}}\):

\[ \hat y=\frac{1}{1+e^{-0.248}} \qquad \boxed{ \hat y \approx 0.5617 } \]

So the network currently predicts about \(56.17\%\) for class \(1\) — while the true label is \(0\). The prediction is on the wrong side, which is why the loss below is fairly large.

(c) Binary cross-entropy loss

\[ L=-\big[\,y\ln\hat y+(1-y)\ln(1-\hat y)\,\big] \]

With \(y=0\) the first term vanishes:

\[ L=-\big[\,0\cdot\ln\hat y+1\cdot\ln(1-\hat y)\,\big]=-\ln(1-\hat y) \] \[ 1-\hat y = 1-0.5617 = 0.4383 \] \[ L=-\ln(0.4383) \]
\[ \boxed{ L \approx 0.8248 } \]

The backward pass — and the one identity that matters

Because the output pairs Sigmoid with BCE, the two derivatives cancel and leave a very clean result:

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{(2)}}=\hat y-y } \]

This quantity is the output delta, \(\delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y\). With \(\hat y=0.5617\) and \(y=0\):

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)} = 0.5617 } \]

You never need to write out \(\sigma'(z)=\sigma(1-\sigma)\) separately for this layer — that factor is already absorbed. This is the single biggest time-saver in the exam.

(d) Gradients of the output weights and bias

\[ z^{(2)}=w^{(2)}_1h_1+w^{(2)}_2h_2+b^{(2)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}}=h\,\delta^{(2)} \] \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix}(0.5617) \]
\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w^{(2)}_1}=0.98(0.5617)\approx 0.5505 \] \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w^{(2)}_2}=0.01(0.5617)\approx 0.00562 \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5505 \\ 0.00562 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(2)}}=\delta^{(2)}=0.5617 } \]

The bias gradient is just \(\delta^{(2)}\) because \(\partial z^{(2)}/\partial b^{(2)}=1\).

(e) Hidden-layer gradients

Propagate the error backwards through \(W^{(2)}\) and then through the ReLU:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)}=\big(W^{(2)}\delta^{(2)}\big)\odot \mathrm{ReLU}'(z^{(1)}) } \]

Step 1 — the ReLU derivative

\[ \mathrm{ReLU}'(z)=\begin{cases} 1, & z>0 \\ 0, & z<0 \end{cases} \qquad z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix} \text{ (both }>0) \] \[ \mathrm{ReLU}'(z^{(1)})=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Step 2 — push the delta back

\[ W^{(2)}\delta^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}(0.5617) =\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} \]

Element-wise multiply by \(\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\), which changes nothing here:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} }\]

Gradient of \(W^{(1)}\)

Keep the orientation in mind: \(W^{(1)}\) is \(3\times2\), so the outer product must be \(x\) (3×1) times \((\delta^{(1)})^{T}\) (1×2).

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}=x\,(\delta^{(1)})^{T} } =\begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 & 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \text{Row 1: } 0.6(0.1685)=0.1011,\qquad 0.6(0.2247)=0.1348 \] \[ \text{Row 2: } 0.9(0.1685)=0.1517,\qquad 0.9(0.2247)=0.2022 \] \[ \text{Row 3: } 0.4(0.1685)=0.0674,\qquad 0.4(0.2247)=0.0899 \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}= \begin{bmatrix} 0.1011 & 0.1348 \\ 0.1517 & 0.2022 \\ 0.0674 & 0.0899 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(1)}}=\delta^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} } \]

(f) Update the output-layer parameters

\[ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{new}}=\theta_{\text{old}}-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta} },\qquad \eta=0.1 \]
\[ w^{(2,\text{new})}_1 = 0.3-0.1(0.5505)=0.3-0.05505 \approx \boxed{0.24495} \] \[ w^{(2,\text{new})}_2 = 0.4-0.1(0.00562)=0.4-0.000562 \approx \boxed{0.39944} \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.24495 \\ 0.39944 \end{bmatrix} } \]
\[ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=-0.05-0.1(0.5617)=-0.05-0.05617 \]
\[ \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx -0.10617 } \]

Sanity check: every parameter moved down, which lowers \(z^{(2)}\), which lowers \(\hat y\) — exactly the right direction when the target is \(y=0\).

(g) Update the hidden-layer parameters

\[ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=W^{(1)}-0.1\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}} \]

Scale the gradient by \(0.1\):

\[ 0.1\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}= \begin{bmatrix} 0.01011 & 0.01348 \\ 0.01517 & 0.02022 \\ 0.00674 & 0.00899 \end{bmatrix} \]

Subtract it from the original \(W^{(1)}\):

\[ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}}= \begin{bmatrix} 0.3-0.01011 & -0.4-0.01348 \\ 0.8-0.01517 & 0.3-0.02022 \\ -0.3-0.00674 & 0.2-0.00899 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.28989 & -0.41348 \\ 0.78483 & 0.27978 \\ -0.30674 & 0.19101 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Hidden bias

\[ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix}-0.1\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} 0.2-0.01685 \\ -0.1-0.02247 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.18315 \\ -0.12247 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Final answer sheet

Forward pass

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ h=\begin{bmatrix} 0.98 \\ 0.01 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ z^{(2)}=0.248 } \qquad \boxed{ \hat y \approx 0.5617 } \qquad \boxed{ L \approx 0.8248 } \]

Backward pass

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y=0.5617 } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5505 \\ 0.00562 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(2)}}=0.5617 } \] \[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1011 & 0.1348 \\ 0.1517 & 0.2022 \\ 0.0674 & 0.0899 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(1)}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1685 \\ 0.2247 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Updated parameters

\[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.24495 \\ 0.39944 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=-0.10617 } \] \[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28989 & -0.41348 \\ 0.78483 & 0.27978 \\ -0.30674 & 0.19101 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.18315 \\ -0.12247 \end{bmatrix} } \]

The pattern to memorize is the pipeline:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Forward}\rightarrow\hat y\rightarrow L\rightarrow\delta^{(2)}\rightarrow\nabla W^{(2)}\rightarrow\delta^{(1)}\rightarrow\nabla W^{(1)}\rightarrow\text{update} } \]

And for Sigmoid + BCE, the key shortcut:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y } \]

That single identity makes the whole backprop much cleaner — it is the same reason Softmax + cross-entropy gives \(\delta=\hat y-y\) in the multi-class case.
