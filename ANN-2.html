The full question

Previous In-Course Exam · Question 1 · 3 → 2 → 2 network

A feed-forward network has 3 inputs, 2 hidden units and 2 outputs. The hidden layer uses ReLU; both output units use Sigmoid. Given:

\[ x=\begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix} \qquad W^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.5 \\ -0.6 & 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.2 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ W^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.4 \\ 0.7 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.05 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

The target is \(y=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\), the loss is categorical cross-entropy, and the learning rate is \(\eta=0.1\).

Answer the following:

  • (a)Compute the hidden-layer activations.
  • (b)Compute the output-layer activations.
  • (c)Compute the categorical cross-entropy loss.
  • (d)Compute the gradients with respect to \(W^{(2)}\) and \(b^{(2)}\).
  • (e)Compute the gradients with respect to \(W^{(1)}\) and \(b^{(1)}\).
  • (f)Update \(W^{(2)}\) and \(b^{(2)}\).
  • (g)Update \(W^{(1)}\) and \(b^{(1)}\).
One oddity in the paper, preserved here deliberately: it labels both outputs Sigmoid yet calls the loss categorical cross-entropy. Those two are not the standard pair. Everything below follows the paper literally — and section the output delta shows exactly what that changes.

The network at a glance

Before solving: shapes and wiring

\(W^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}\) (3 inputs, 2 hidden) and now \(W^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}\) (2 hidden, 2 outputs). Both are stored input-by-output, so both need transposing in the forward pass:

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=(W^{(1)})^{T}x+b^{(1)} } \qquad \boxed{ h=\mathrm{ReLU}(z^{(1)}) } \] \[ \boxed{ z^{(2)}=(W^{(2)})^{T}h+b^{(2)} } \qquad \boxed{ \hat y_k=\sigma(z^{(2)}_k) } \] 
   x (3)              h (2)              y^ (2)
  ┌─────┐   W(1)ᵀ    ┌──────┐   W(2)ᵀ   ┌─────┐
  │ 0.6 │ ─────────► │ ReLU │ ────────► │  σ  │ ──► y^₁        y = [1, 0]ᵀ
  │ 0.9 │  + b(1)    │ ReLU │  + b(2)   │  σ  │ ──► y^₂   ──►  CCE  ──► L
  │ 0.4 │            └──────┘           └─────┘
  └─────┘
       3×2                   2×2            two INDEPENDENT sigmoids
                                            (no shared normaliser)

The single structural difference from the \(3\rightarrow2\rightarrow1\) paper: the output layer is now a matrix, not a vector, so \(\delta^{(2)}\) is a 2-vector and \(\partial L/\partial W^{(2)}\) is \(2\times2\).

(a) Hidden-layer activations

\[ (W^{(1)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & 0.2 & -0.6 \\ -0.3 & 0.5 & 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & 0.2 & -0.6 \\ -0.3 & 0.5 & 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Hidden neuron 1

\[ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.4(0.6)+0.2(0.9)-0.6(0.4)+0.1 \] \[ = 0.24+0.18-0.24+0.1 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.28 } \]

Hidden neuron 2

\[ z^{(1)}_2 = -0.3(0.6)+0.5(0.9)+0.1(0.4)-0.2 \] \[ = -0.18+0.45+0.04-0.2 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.11 } \]

Apply ReLU. Both pre-activations are positive, so both pass through unchanged:

\[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{ h=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Note for part (e): no ReLU unit is dead, so \(\mathrm{ReLU}'(z^{(1)})=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\) later.

(b) Output activations

\[ (W^{(2)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.7 \\ -0.4 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ z^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.7 \\ -0.4 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix} 0.05 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Output 1

\[ z^{(2)}_1 = 0.5(0.28)+0.7(0.11)+0.05 = 0.14+0.077+0.05 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)}_1 = 0.267 } \]

Output 2

\[ z^{(2)}_2 = -0.4(0.28)+0.3(0.11)-0.1 = -0.112+0.033-0.1 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)}_2 = -0.179 } \]

Apply the sigmoid to each output independently:

\[ \hat y_1=\sigma(0.267)\approx 0.5664 \qquad \hat y_2=\sigma(-0.179)\approx 0.4554 \] \[ \boxed{ \hat y \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5664 \\ 0.4554 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Notice that these do not sum to 1:

\[ 0.5664+0.4554 = 1.0218 \neq 1 \]

That is not an arithmetic slip — it is the direct consequence of two independent sigmoids instead of a Softmax. Softmax divides by a shared normaliser, so its outputs are forced to sum to 1; sigmoids have no such coupling. Write this observation down in the exam: it shows you understand the difference rather than having mis-added.

(c) Categorical cross-entropy loss

\[ L = -\sum_k y_k\ln\hat y_k \]

With \(y=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}\), the second term is multiplied by zero and drops out:

\[ L = -\big[\,1\cdot\ln(0.5664)+0\cdot\ln(0.4554)\,\big] = -\ln(0.5664) \]
\[ \boxed{ L \approx 0.5685 } \]

Only the target class contributes. Keep that in mind for the next section — it is precisely why the second output ends up with no gradient at all.

The output delta — do not reach for \(\hat y-y\) here

In the \(3\rightarrow2\rightarrow1\) paper the output was Sigmoid + BCE, and the two derivatives cancelled to give \(\delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y\). The same shortcut holds for Softmax + CCE. This paper pairs Sigmoid with CCE, which is neither, so the cancellation is only partial. Derive it:

\[ L=-\sum_k y_k\ln\hat y_k, \qquad \hat y_k=\sigma(z_k), \qquad \sigma'(z_k)=\hat y_k(1-\hat y_k) \]

By the chain rule, for one output \(k\):

\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_k} = \underbrace{-\frac{y_k}{\hat y_k}}_{\partial L/\partial\hat y_k}\cdot \underbrace{\hat y_k(1-\hat y_k)}_{\partial\hat y_k/\partial z_k} \]

The \(\hat y_k\) cancels — but only one of them, leaving the \(y_k\) factor in place:

\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_k} = -y_k(1-\hat y_k) \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}_k = y_k(\hat y_k-1) } \]

Evaluate for both outputs

\[ \delta^{(2)}_1 = y_1(\hat y_1-1) = 1(0.5664-1) \qquad \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}_1 = -0.4336 } \] \[ \delta^{(2)}_2 = y_2(\hat y_2-1) = 0\cdot(0.4554-1) \qquad \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}_2 = 0 } \]
\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Compare with the shortcut you were not allowed to use here:

\[ \hat y - y = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5664-1 \\ 0.4554-0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0.4554 \end{bmatrix} \qquad\text{vs.}\qquad \delta^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

The target output happens to agree; the non-target output does not. Under this literal reading, \(\delta^{(2)}_2=0\), which means the loss gives the network no instruction to push \(\hat y_2\) down — output 2 simply never learns. That dead gradient is exactly the defect Softmax + CCE exists to fix, because there the shared normaliser makes lowering \(\hat y_2\) part of raising \(\hat y_1\).

\[ \boxed{ \text{Identify the activation and the loss first. Only then pick the } \delta \text{ formula.} } \]

(d) Gradients of the output weights and bias

Since \(z^{(2)}_k=\sum_i W^{(2)}_{ik}h_i+b^{(2)}_k\), we get \(\partial z^{(2)}_k/\partial W^{(2)}_{ik}=h_i\), so the gradient is the outer product:

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}}=h\,(\delta^{(2)})^{T} } = \begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \text{Row 1: } 0.28(-0.4336)=-0.1214,\qquad 0.28(0)=0 \] \[ \text{Row 2: } 0.11(-0.4336)=-0.0477,\qquad 0.11(0)=0 \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.1214 & 0 \\ -0.0477 & 0 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(2)}} = \delta^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} } \]

The entire second column is zero — every weight feeding output 2, and its bias, receives nothing. That is the dead gradient from the previous section showing up in the numbers.

(e) Hidden-layer gradients

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)}=\big(W^{(2)}\delta^{(2)}\big)\odot\mathrm{ReLU}'(z^{(1)}) } \]

Step 1 — the ReLU derivative

\[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix} \text{ (both }>0) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathrm{ReLU}'(z^{(1)})=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Step 2 — push the delta back through \(W^{(2)}\)

Note this uses \(W^{(2)}\) itself, not its transpose — going backwards undoes the transpose taken in the forward pass:

\[ W^{(2)}\delta^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.4 \\ 0.7 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \text{Hidden 1: } 0.5(-0.4336)+(-0.4)(0) = -0.2168 \] \[ \text{Hidden 2: } 0.7(-0.4336)+0.3(0) = -0.3036 \]

Element-wise multiply by \(\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\), which changes nothing here:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 \\ -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Gradient of \(W^{(1)}\)

\(W^{(1)}\) is \(3\times2\), so the outer product is \(x\) (3×1) times \((\delta^{(1)})^{T}\) (1×2):

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}=x\,(\delta^{(1)})^{T} } = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.9 \\ 0.4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 & -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \text{Row 1: } 0.6(-0.2168)=-0.1301,\qquad 0.6(-0.3036)=-0.1821 \] \[ \text{Row 2: } 0.9(-0.2168)=-0.1951,\qquad 0.9(-0.3036)=-0.2732 \] \[ \text{Row 3: } 0.4(-0.2168)=-0.0867,\qquad 0.4(-0.3036)=-0.1214 \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.1301 & -0.1821 \\ -0.1951 & -0.2732 \\ -0.0867 & -0.1214 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(1)}} = \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 \\ -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Every entry is negative, so every hidden parameter will move up — which raises \(h\), raises \(z^{(2)}_1\), and raises \(\hat y_1\) toward its target of 1. That is the right direction.

(f) Update the output-layer parameters

\[ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{new}}=\theta_{\text{old}}-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta} },\qquad \eta=0.1 \]
\[ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.4 \\ 0.7 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix} -0.1\begin{bmatrix} -0.1214 & 0 \\ -0.0477 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5+0.01214 & -0.4-0 \\ 0.7+0.00477 & 0.3-0 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5121 & -0.4000 \\ 0.7048 & 0.3000 \end{bmatrix} } \]
\[ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.05 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix}-0.1\begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} 0.05+0.04336 \\ -0.1-0 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.0934 \\ -0.1000 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Column 2 of \(W^{(2)}\) and \(b^{(2)}_2\) are unchanged after a full training step. Write the unchanged values out anyway — they are part of the answer, and stating why they did not move is worth marks.

(g) Update the hidden-layer parameters

Scale the \(W^{(1)}\) gradient by \(\eta=0.1\):

\[ 0.1\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}}= \begin{bmatrix} -0.01301 & -0.01821 \\ -0.01951 & -0.02732 \\ -0.00867 & -0.01214 \end{bmatrix} \]

Subtract from \(W^{(1)}\) — subtracting a negative means every entry increases:

\[ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}}= \begin{bmatrix} 0.4+0.01301 & -0.3+0.01821 \\ 0.2+0.01951 & 0.5+0.02732 \\ -0.6+0.00867 & 0.1+0.01214 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4130 & -0.2818 \\ 0.2195 & 0.5273 \\ -0.5913 & 0.1121 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Hidden bias

\[ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.2 \end{bmatrix}-0.1\begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 \\ -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} 0.1+0.02168 \\ -0.2+0.03036 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.1217 \\ -0.1696 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Final answer sheet

Forward pass

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ h=\begin{bmatrix} 0.28 \\ 0.11 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.267 \\ -0.179 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ \hat y \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5664 \\ 0.4554 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ L \approx 0.5685 } \]

Backward pass

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(2)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.1214 & 0 \\ -0.0477 & 0 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(2)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.4336 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ \delta^{(1)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 \\ -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(1)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.1301 & -0.1821 \\ -0.1951 & -0.2732 \\ -0.0867 & -0.1214 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial b^{(1)}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} -0.2168 \\ -0.3036 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Updated parameters

\[ \boxed{ W^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5121 & -0.4000 \\ 0.7048 & 0.3000 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(2)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.0934 \\ -0.1000 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \boxed{ W^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4130 & -0.2818 \\ 0.2195 & 0.5273 \\ -0.5913 & 0.1121 \end{bmatrix} } \qquad \boxed{ b^{(1)}_{\text{new}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.1217 \\ -0.1696 \end{bmatrix} } \]

What this problem is actually testing

The pipeline is unchanged from ANN-1:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Forward}\rightarrow\hat y\rightarrow L\rightarrow\delta^{(2)}\rightarrow\nabla W^{(2)}\rightarrow\delta^{(1)}\rightarrow\nabla W^{(1)}\rightarrow\text{update} } \]

What changes is the single step in the middle. You cannot blindly use \(\delta=\hat y-y\) for every output/loss pair — first identify the activation and the loss, then pick the formula:

\[ \text{Sigmoid + BCE} \;\Rightarrow\; \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y \] \[ \text{Softmax + CCE} \;\Rightarrow\; \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y \] \[ \text{Sigmoid + CCE (this paper)} \;\Rightarrow\; \delta^{(2)}_k=y_k(\hat y_k-1) \]

The next ANN questions switch to the clean pairing tanh hidden + Softmax output + CCE, where the shortcut \(\delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y\) is back and fully justified.
