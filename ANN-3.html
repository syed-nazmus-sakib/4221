The full question

Current In-Course Exam · Question 1 · 2 → 3 → 2 network · forward pass only

A feed-forward network has 2 inputs, 3 Tanh hidden neurons and 2 Softmax output neurons. Given:

\[ x=\begin{bmatrix} 1.0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad y=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ W^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.4 & 0.2 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.2 \\ 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ W^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.3 \\ 0.4 & -0.2 \\ 0.3 & -0.4 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

The paper explicitly says to use \((W^{(1)})^{T}\) and \((W^{(2)})^{T}\) when calculating the pre-activations.

Answer the following:

  • (a)Compute the hidden pre-activations \(z^{(1)}\) and the Tanh activations \(h\).
  • (b)Compute the output pre-activations \(z^{(2)}\) and the Softmax probabilities \(\hat y\).
  • (c)Compute the categorical cross-entropy loss.
  • (d)If the true class were instead class 2, i.e. \(y=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}\), compute the new loss.

Equations for this pairing:

\[ z^{(1)}=(W^{(1)})^{T}x+b^{(1)} \qquad h=\tanh(z^{(1)}) \] \[ z^{(2)}=(W^{(2)})^{T}h+b^{(2)} \qquad \hat y_k=\frac{e^{z^{(2)}_k}}{\sum_j e^{z^{(2)}_j}} \] \[ L=-\sum_k y_k\ln\hat y_k \]

Before solving: shapes and wiring

\(W^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times3}\) (2 inputs → 3 hidden) and \(W^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}\) (3 hidden → 2 outputs). Both are stored input-by-output, so both get transposed in the forward pass — exactly as the paper instructs:

\[ (W^{(1)})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times2} \;\text{ times }\; x\in\mathbb{R}^{2} \;\Rightarrow\; z^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{3} \;\checkmark \] \[ (W^{(2)})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times3} \;\text{ times }\; h\in\mathbb{R}^{3} \;\Rightarrow\; z^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2} \;\checkmark \] 
    x (2)               h (3)                y^ (2)
   ┌─────┐   W(1)ᵀ    ┌──────┐   W(2)ᵀ     ┌──────────┐
   │ 1.0 │ ─────────► │ tanh │ ──────────► │          │ ─► y^₁ = 0.733
   │ 0.5 │  + b(1)    │ tanh │   + b(2)    │ Softmax  │ ─► y^₂ = 0.267
   └─────┘            │ tanh │             │ (shared  │
                      └──────┘             │  denom.) │ ─► sums to 1
        2×3                    3×2         └──────────┘
                                                 │
                                        y = [1,0]ᵀ ─► CCE ─► L

Two things separate this from ANN-2: the hidden units are tanh (output in \((-1,1)\), so they can be negative — no dead units), and the outputs share a single denominator, so they are forced to sum to 1. That coupling is what makes Softmax the correct partner for categorical cross-entropy.

(a) Hidden layer — pre-activations and Tanh

\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=(W^{(1)})^{T}x+b^{(1)} } \qquad (W^{(1)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.4 \\ 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.2 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.4 \\ 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.2 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.2 \\ 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Hidden neuron 1

\[ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.3(1.0)+0.4(0.5)+0.1 = 0.3+0.2+0.1 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_1 = 0.60 } \]

Hidden neuron 2

\[ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.6(1.0)+0.2(0.5)-0.2 = 0.6+0.1-0.2 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_2 = 0.50 } \]

Hidden neuron 3

\[ z^{(1)}_3 = 0.2(1.0)+0.8(0.5)+0.1 = 0.2+0.4+0.1 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(1)}_3 = 0.70 } \]
\[ \boxed{ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.60 \\ 0.50 \\ 0.70 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Apply Tanh

\[ h=\tanh(z^{(1)}), \qquad \tanh(z)=\frac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{e^{z}+e^{-z}} \] \[ h_1=\tanh(0.60)\approx 0.537 \qquad h_2=\tanh(0.50)\approx 0.462 \qquad h_3=\tanh(0.70)\approx 0.604 \]
\[ \boxed{ h \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.537 \\ 0.462 \\ 0.604 \end{bmatrix} } \]

All three are positive here only because all three pre-activations were positive. Unlike ReLU, tanh would have passed a negative \(z\) through as a negative activation rather than zeroing it — so there is no "dead unit" case to check.

(b) Output layer — logits and Softmax

\[ \boxed{ z^{(2)}=(W^{(2)})^{T}h+b^{(2)} } \qquad (W^{(2)})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.4 & 0.3 \\ 0.3 & -0.2 & -0.4 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ z^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.4 & 0.3 \\ 0.3 & -0.2 & -0.4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.537 \\ 0.462 \\ 0.604 \end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Output neuron 1

\[ 0.5(0.537)=0.2685 \qquad 0.4(0.462)=0.1848 \qquad 0.3(0.604)=0.1812 \] \[ z^{(2)}_1 = 0.2685+0.1848+0.1812+0.1 = 0.7345 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)}_1 \approx 0.735 } \]

Output neuron 2

\[ 0.3(0.537)=0.1611 \qquad -0.2(0.462)=-0.0924 \qquad -0.4(0.604)=-0.2416 \] \[ z^{(2)}_2 = 0.1611-0.0924-0.2416-0.1 = -0.2729 \qquad \boxed{ z^{(2)}_2 \approx -0.273 } \]
\[ \boxed{ z^{(2)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.735 \\ -0.273 \end{bmatrix} } \]

Apply Softmax

Turn the logits into class probabilities. For two classes:

\[ \hat y_1=\frac{e^{z_1}}{e^{z_1}+e^{z_2}} \qquad \hat y_2=\frac{e^{z_2}}{e^{z_1}+e^{z_2}} \]
\[ e^{0.735}\approx 2.085 \qquad e^{-0.273}\approx 0.761 \] \[ \text{denominator} = 2.085+0.761 = 2.846 \]
\[ \hat y_1=\frac{2.085}{2.846}\approx 0.733 \qquad \hat y_2=\frac{0.761}{2.846}\approx 0.267 \]
\[ \boxed{ \hat y \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.733 \\ 0.267 \end{bmatrix} }, \qquad 0.733+0.267=1 \;\checkmark \]

The sum-to-one check is free marks — and it is a genuine check, unlike in ANN-2 where independent sigmoids summed to \(1.0218\). If your Softmax outputs do not sum to 1, you have an arithmetic error.

Exam tip. Softmax is unchanged if you subtract the same constant from every logit: \(\dfrac{e^{z_k-c}}{\sum_j e^{z_j-c}}=\dfrac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}\). Taking \(c=\max_j z_j\) keeps the exponentials small and easy to handle by hand. Here \(c=0.735\) would give \(e^{0}=1\) and \(e^{-1.008}\approx0.365\), and \(1/1.365\approx0.733\) — same answer, easier arithmetic.

(c) Categorical cross-entropy loss

\[ L=-\sum_k y_k\ln\hat y_k, \qquad y=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

The one-hot target zeroes out every term except the true class:

\[ L = -\big[\,1\cdot\ln(0.733)+0\cdot\ln(0.267)\,\big] = -\ln(0.733) \]
\[ \boxed{ L \approx 0.311 } \]

Why is this loss fairly small?

Because the correct class is class 1, and the network assigned it \(\boxed{73.3\%}\) probability. Cross-entropy is just the negative log of the probability given to the right answer, so more confidence in the correct class means a lower loss:

\[ \hat y_{\text{correct}}=1.0 \;\Rightarrow\; L=0 \qquad \hat y_{\text{correct}}=0.733 \;\Rightarrow\; L=0.311 \qquad \hat y_{\text{correct}}=0.5 \;\Rightarrow\; L=0.693 \]

\(0.693=\ln 2\) is the loss of a coin flip on two classes — a useful reference point. Anything below it means the network is doing better than guessing on this example.

(d) Suppose the true class is class 2

\[ y=\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Importantly, do not redo the forward pass. The weights, the input and therefore the predictions are all unchanged — only the label changed:

\[ \hat y = \begin{bmatrix} 0.733 \\ 0.267 \end{bmatrix} \quad\text{(same as before)} \]
\[ L' = -\big[\,0\cdot\ln(0.733)+1\cdot\ln(0.267)\,\big] = -\ln(0.267) \]
\[ \boxed{ L' \approx 1.322 } \]

On rounding: the model answer quotes \(\approx1.322\). Taking \(-\ln(0.267)\) literally gives \(1.3205\), and carrying full precision through the whole forward pass gives \(1.3189\). All three agree to 2 decimal places — if you get \(1.32\), you are right, and it is worth writing which rounding you carried.

Comparing the two losses

True classTarget \(y\)Probability given to itLoss
Class 1\([1,0]^{T}\)\(0.733\)\(L=-\ln(0.733)\approx 0.311\)
Class 2\([0,1]^{T}\)\(0.267\)\(L'=-\ln(0.267)\approx 1.322\)

The loss more than quadruples. The reason is that the network predicted \(P(\text{class }1)=0.733\) but only \(P(\text{class }2)=0.267\) — so if class 2 were truly correct, the model would be badly wrong, and cross-entropy punishes confident mistakes steeply.

\[ \boxed{ L=-\ln\big(\text{probability assigned to the correct class}\big) } \]

Both answers come from the same \(\hat y\). Part (d) is testing whether you realise the forward pass does not depend on the label at all — the label only enters at the loss.

Final answer sheet

\[ \textbf{Hidden pre-activation: }\; \boxed{ z^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix} 0.60 \\ 0.50 \\ 0.70 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \textbf{Tanh activation: }\; \boxed{ h \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.537 \\ 0.462 \\ 0.604 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \textbf{Output logits: }\; \boxed{ z^{(2)} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.735 \\ -0.273 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \textbf{Softmax: }\; \boxed{ \hat y \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.733 \\ 0.267 \end{bmatrix} } \] \[ \textbf{Loss for } y=[1,0]^{T}: \; \boxed{ L \approx 0.311 } \qquad \textbf{Loss for } y=[0,1]^{T}: \; \boxed{ L' \approx 1.322 } \]

The new pattern to memorize

\[ \boxed{ x \rightarrow z^{(1)} \rightarrow \tanh \rightarrow z^{(2)} \rightarrow \text{Softmax} \rightarrow \text{CCE} } \]

And for a one-hot target, categorical cross-entropy collapses to a single logarithm:

\[ \boxed{ L=-\ln\big(\text{probability assigned to the correct class}\big) } \] \[ \hat y_{\text{correct}}=0.733 \;\Rightarrow\; L=-\ln(0.733)\approx0.311 \] \[ \hat y_{\text{correct}}=0.267 \;\Rightarrow\; L=-\ln(0.267)\approx1.322 \]

Next is the retake \(3\rightarrow3\rightarrow3\) problem. It uses this same Tanh + Softmax + CCE forward pass, but adds the multiclass backprop result — and because the pairing is now the standard one, the clean shortcut is finally justified:

\[ \boxed{ \delta^{(2)}=\hat y-y } \]

Contrast with ANN-2, where Sigmoid + CCE forced \(\delta^{(2)}_k=y_k(\hat y_k-1)\) instead. Identify the activation and the loss first, then pick the formula.
