The full question

Previous In-Course Exam · LSTM cell calculation · 10 marks

Consider an LSTM cell with hidden-state dimension 2. At time step \(t\) you are given the previous hidden state, the previous cell state, and the current input:

\[ h_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad C_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad x_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix}. \]

The gates are computed without concatenating \(h_{t-1}\) and \(x_t\). Instead, each gate has a separate weight matrix for the hidden state and for the input, with the following values:

\[ \textbf{Forget: }\; W_f^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -1.0 \\ -0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix},\; W_f^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.6 \\ 0.8 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix},\; b_f = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \textbf{Input: }\; W_i^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix},\; W_i^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 & 0.4 \\ -0.2 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix},\; b_i = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \textbf{Candidate: }\; W_c^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & -0.2 \\ -0.4 & 0.1 \end{bmatrix},\; W_c^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.1 \\ 0.2 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix},\; b_c = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \textbf{Output: }\; W_o^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.2 \\ -0.6 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix},\; W_o^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.7 \\ 0.5 & -0.1 \end{bmatrix},\; b_o = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix} \]

Answer the following:

  • (a)Draw the LSTM cell, labelling the forget gate, the input gate, the candidate cell state, the cell state and the output gate.
  • (b)Compute the forget gate \(f_t\).
  • (c)Compute the input gate \(i_t\).
  • (d)Compute the candidate cell state \(\tilde C_t\).
  • (e)Compute the updated cell state \(C_t\).
  • (f)Compute the output gate \(o_t\) and the final hidden state \(h_t\).

Use \(\sigma(z)=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-z}}\) and \(\tanh\) where appropriate; round to 4 decimal places.

Standard equations implied by those gate names:

\[ f_t=\sigma(W_f^{(h)}h_{t-1}+W_f^{(x)}x_t+b_f) \] \[ i_t=\sigma(W_i^{(h)}h_{t-1}+W_i^{(x)}x_t+b_i) \] \[ \tilde C_t=\tanh(W_c^{(h)}h_{t-1}+W_c^{(x)}x_t+b_c) \] \[ C_t=f_t\odot C_{t-1}+i_t\odot\tilde C_t \] \[ o_t=\sigma(W_o^{(h)}h_{t-1}+W_o^{(x)}x_t+b_o) \] \[ h_t=o_t\odot\tanh(C_t) \]

where \(\odot\) means element-wise multiplication.

Before solving: read the cell once

Every part below is one region of this single picture. Get the geography straight now and each calculation becomes a lookup rather than a memory test.

Three things to notice before any arithmetic:

  1. The top line is memory. \(C_{t-1}\) enters at the top-left and \(C_t\) leaves at the top-right. It is only ever touched twice: once by a \(\times\) (forget) and once by a \(+\) (write).
  2. The bottom line is inputs. \(h_{t-1}\) and \(x_t\) feed all four boxes — \(f_t, i_t, \tilde C_t, o_t\) — which is why all four have the same \(W^{(h)}(\cdot)+W^{(x)}(\cdot)+b\) shape and differ only in their weights.
  3. Three boxes use \(\sigma\) (they are valves, output in \((0,1)\)); only \(\tilde C_t\) uses \(\tanh\) (it is content, output in \((-1,1)\)).

The fixed solution order

Think of it as \(\boxed{h_{t-1},x_t}\) feeding four computations: \(f_t,\; i_t,\; \tilde C_t,\; o_t.\) Then:

\[ \boxed{ C_t = f_t\odot C_{t-1} + i_t\odot\tilde C_t } \]

and finally:

\[ \boxed{ h_t=o_t\odot\tanh(C_t) } \]

So the entire numerical question always follows:

\[ \boxed{ f_t \rightarrow i_t \rightarrow \tilde C_t \rightarrow C_t \rightarrow o_t \rightarrow h_t } \]

That order is worth memorizing — it is the order the diagram is wired in, left to right.

(a) Draw the LSTM cell

This part is the diagram, so here is the whole cell again — every path live. In the exam, reproduce these five labelled pieces and the two lines they sit between.

A hand-drawable version of the same thing:

                 ┌──────── f_t ────────┐
C_(t-1) ─────────────── × ─────────────┐
                                       │
                                       + ─────── C_t ── tanh ── × ── h_t
                                       │                    ↑
                 ┌──── i_t ──┐         │                    │
h_(t-1), x_t ────┤            ×────────┘                   o_t
                 └─ C~_t ────┘

Interpretation: \(f_t\) how much old memory to keep · \(i_t\) how much new candidate memory to write · \(\tilde C_t\) proposed new information · \(C_t\) updated memory · \(o_t\) how much memory to expose as hidden state.

(b) Forget gate \(f_t\)

Where this lives in the cell — the \(f_t\) box and the \(\times\) it drives on the memory line:

\[ W_f^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -1.0 \\ -0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \quad W_f^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.6 \\ 0.8 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix} \quad b_f = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix}. \] \[ z_f = W_f^{(h)}h_{t-1} + W_f^{(x)}x_t + b_f. \]

Hidden-state contribution

\[ W_f^{(h)}h_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -1 \\ -0.3 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \text{First: } 0.4(0.3)+(-1)(-0.5) = 0.12+0.50 = 0.62. \] \[ \text{Second: } -0.3(0.3)+0.2(-0.5) = -0.09-0.10 = -0.19. \] \[ = \begin{bmatrix} 0.62 \\ -0.19 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Input contribution

\[ W_f^{(x)}x_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.6 \\ 0.8 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5(0.8)+0.6(0.2) \\ 0.8(0.8)-0.3(0.2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.52 \\ 0.58 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Now add everything:

\[ z_f = \begin{bmatrix} 0.62 \\ -0.19 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0.52 \\ 0.58 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ -0.1 \end{bmatrix} = \boxed{ \begin{bmatrix} 1.34 \\ 0.29 \end{bmatrix}} \]
\[ f_t = \sigma(z_f) \quad \boxed{ f_t \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.7925 \\ 0.5720 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Reading it back on the diagram: \(0.7925\) means unit 1 keeps about 79% of its old memory, unit 2 keeps about 57%. Nothing is written yet — that is parts (c) and (d).

(c) Input gate \(i_t\)

Same input line, different box: \(i_t\) feeds the middle \(\times\), the one that gates the new content.

\[ W_i^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & -0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix},\quad W_i^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 & 0.4 \\ -0.2 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix},\quad b_i = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.1 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Hidden contribution \(W_i^{(h)}h_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.27 \\ -0.14 \end{bmatrix}.\) Input contribution \(W_i^{(x)}x_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.16 \\ -0.06 \end{bmatrix}.\)

\[ z_i = \begin{bmatrix} 0.27 \\ -0.14 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0.16 \\ -0.06 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.1 \end{bmatrix} = \boxed{ \begin{bmatrix} 0.43 \\ -0.10 \end{bmatrix}} \]
\[ \boxed{ i_t = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma(0.43) \\ \sigma(-0.10) \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.6059 \\ 0.4750 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Still a valve, not content — \(i_t\) alone cannot change memory until it is multiplied by \(\tilde C_t\) at that \(\times\).

(d) Candidate cell state \(\tilde C_t\)

The other half of that \(\times\): the only tanh box on the input side — the content being proposed.

\[ W_c^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & -0.2 \\ -0.4 & 0.1 \end{bmatrix} \quad W_c^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.1 \\ 0.2 & 0.3 \end{bmatrix},\quad b_c = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Hidden: \(W_c^{(h)}h_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.19 \\ -0.17 \end{bmatrix}.\) Input: \(W_c^{(x)}x_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.42 \\ 0.22 \end{bmatrix}.\)

\[ z_c = \begin{bmatrix} 0.19 \\ -0.17 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0.42 \\ 0.22 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix} = \boxed{ \begin{bmatrix} 0.61 \\ 0.10 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Candidate uses tanh, not sigmoid:

\[ \tilde C_t = \tanh(z_c) \quad \boxed{ \tilde C_t \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5441 \\ 0.0997 \end{bmatrix}} \]

This is the single most common slip in the exam — using \(\sigma\) here would force the proposed content to be positive, which is wrong.

(e) Updated cell state \(C_t\)

Now the two streams meet: the kept old memory arrives from the forget \(\times\), the new content from the middle \(\times\), and the \(+\) merges them into \(C_t\).

\[ C_t = f_t\odot C_{t-1} + i_t\odot\tilde C_t. \]

Retained old memory (the \(\times\) on the top line):

\[ f_t\odot C_{t-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.7925 \\ 0.5720 \end{bmatrix} \odot \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ -0.3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.07925 \\ -0.17160 \end{bmatrix}. \]

New memory (the middle \(\times\)):

\[ i_t\odot\tilde C_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6059 \\ 0.4750 \end{bmatrix} \odot \begin{bmatrix} 0.5441 \\ 0.0997 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.32967 \\ 0.04734 \end{bmatrix}. \]
\[ \boxed{ C_t \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4089 \\ -0.1243 \end{bmatrix}} \]

\(C_t\) is final here — it leaves the cell along the top line un-squashed. The \(\tanh\) in part (f) is applied to a copy of it, not to the memory itself.

(f) Output gate \(o_t\) + hidden \(h_t\)

Last valve on the input line. It reads \(h_{t-1},x_t\) — not \(C_t\) — and feeds the right-hand \(\times\).

\[ W_o^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.2 \\ -0.6 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix} \quad W_o^{(x)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.7 \\ 0.5 & -0.1 \end{bmatrix},\quad b_o = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Hidden: \(\begin{bmatrix} 0.25 \\ -0.38 \end{bmatrix}.\) Input: \(\begin{bmatrix} 0.38 \\ 0.38 \end{bmatrix}.\)

\[ z_o = \begin{bmatrix} 0.25 \\ -0.38 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0.38 \\ 0.38 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix} = \boxed{ \begin{bmatrix} 0.63 \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix}} \]
\[ \boxed{ o_t \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.6525 \\ 0.5125 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Final hidden state \(h_t\)

The last stretch of the diagram: \(C_t\) is tapped off the memory line, squashed by \(\tanh\), multiplied by \(o_t\), and leaves as \(h_t\).

\[ h_t = o_t\odot\tanh(C_t). \quad C_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4089 \\ -0.1243 \end{bmatrix}. \]
\[ \tanh(C_t) \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.3876 \\ -0.1236 \end{bmatrix}. \]
\[ h_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6525 \\ 0.5125 \end{bmatrix} \odot \begin{bmatrix} 0.3876 \\ -0.1236 \end{bmatrix} \quad \boxed{ h_t \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.2529 \\ -0.0634 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Both outputs of the cell are now known: \(C_t\) leaves along the top, \(h_t\) leaves on the right. Together they are the state passed to \(t+1\).

Final answer sheet

\[ \boxed{ f_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.7925 \\ 0.5720 \end{bmatrix}} \quad \boxed{ i_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6059 \\ 0.4750 \end{bmatrix}} \quad \boxed{ \tilde C_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5441 \\ 0.0997 \end{bmatrix}} \] \[ \boxed{ C_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4089 \\ -0.1243 \end{bmatrix}} \quad \boxed{ o_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6525 \\ 0.5125 \end{bmatrix}} \quad \boxed{ h_t = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2529 \\ -0.0634 \end{bmatrix}} \]

Only one LSTM numerical algorithm: \(\boxed{f\rightarrow i\rightarrow\tilde C\rightarrow C\rightarrow o\rightarrow h}\). The next previous-final question uses essentially the same gate matrices but changes \(h_{t-1}, C_{t-1}, x_t\).
